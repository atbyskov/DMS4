\section{Sequential Linear Programming -- Implementation Details}
\label{sec:SLP_appendix}

This appendix expands the summary given in Section~\ref{sec:SLP}.
The solver is a Python port of the merit-function variant of the
MATLAB framework of \citet{sorensen2026fminslp}, coupled to the
MAPDL beam model.  Each subsection below corresponds to one bullet
of the main-chapter summary: the linearisation and
finite-difference gradients (Section~\ref{sec:slpA:linearise}), the
slack-relaxed linear subproblem (Section~\ref{sec:slpA:lp}), the
merit function (Section~\ref{sec:slpA:merit}), the filter and
sufficient-decrease acceptance test (Section~\ref{sec:slpA:accept})
and the adaptive move limits acting as a trust region
(Section~\ref{sec:slpA:movelim}).  The adaptive slack penalty
(Section~\ref{sec:slpA:penalty}) and termination criteria
(Section~\ref{sec:slpA:termination}) close the description, and the
full iteration is collected in Algorithm~\ref{alg:slp}.
Section~\ref{sec:slpA:settings} lists the numerical values used in
the present study.

\subsection{Problem statement and sign convention}
\label{sec:slpA:problem}

The minimization problem is stated with non-negative inequality
constraints,
\begin{align}
    \min_{x \in \mathbb{R}^n} \;\; f(x),
    \qquad
    \text{s.t.} \;\;
    c_i(x) \geq 0,\; i = 1,\dots,m,
    \qquad
    x^L \leq x \leq x^U,
    \label{eq:appA_problem}
\end{align}
where $f$ is the structural mass returned by MAPDL and $c$ collects
the utilization, cross-section class, eigenvalue and segment-mass
constraints of the design problem.  The reference solver of
\citet{sorensen2026fminslp} works in the dual convention
$g(x) \leq 0$, and the two are bridged in the implementation by a
single sign flip applied at the constraint callback,
\begin{align}
    g(x) \;=\; -c(x).
    \label{eq:appA_sign_flip}
\end{align}
All algorithmic quantities below are therefore defined in
$g(x) \leq 0$.

\subsection{Linearisation and finite-difference gradients}
\label{sec:slpA:linearise}

At every iteration~$k$ the nonlinear objective and constraints are
expanded to first order around the current iterate $x_k$,
\begin{align}
    f(x_k + \Delta x) &\approx f(x_k) + \nabla f(x_k)^{\!\top}\Delta x,
    \label{eq:appA_lin_f}
    \\
    g(x_k + \Delta x) &\approx g(x_k) + J(x_k)\, \Delta x,
    \label{eq:appA_lin_g}
\end{align}
with $J(x) = \nabla g(x)^{\!\top}$ the constraint Jacobian.  Because
the MAPDL model is a black box, $\nabla f$ and $J$ are obtained by
forward finite differences with the absolute step
\begin{align}
    h_i \;=\; h_{\text{rel}}\,\cdot\,
              \max\bigl(1,\,|x_{k,i}|\bigr),
    \label{eq:appA_fd}
\end{align}
with $h_{\text{rel}}$ a fixed relative step size.  Each gradient
evaluation costs $n$ extra FEA calls per outer iteration and is the
dominant cost of the run.

\subsection{The slack-relaxed linear subproblem}
\label{sec:slpA:lp}

A direct linearisation gives the LP
\begin{align*}
    \min_{\Delta x}\;\;
    \nabla f(x_k)^{\!\top}\Delta x
    \quad \text{s.t.} \quad
    g(x_k) + J(x_k)\,\Delta x \leq 0,
    \quad
    x^{L,\text{cur}} \leq x_k + \Delta x \leq x^{U,\text{cur}},
\end{align*}
which is infeasible whenever $x_k$ violates any linearised
constraint.  Because the initial LT1292 design is typically
infeasible against several stability checks, the inequalities are
relaxed by a non-negative slack vector
$y \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^m$,
\begin{align}
    g_i(x) - y_i \leq 0, \qquad i = 1,\dots,m,
    \label{eq:appA_slack}
\end{align}
initialised at $y_i = \max(0,\,g_i(x_0))$ and bounded above by
$10^{6}\,\max(1,\,\|g(x_0)\|_\infty)$ so the LP polytope stays
bounded.  With the augmented decision vector $z = [x,\,y]$, the
linear subproblem solved at every outer iteration is
\begin{align}
    \min_{\Delta z} \;\;
        & \nabla_z \Phi(x_k, y_k)^{\!\top} \Delta z,
    \label{eq:appA_lp_obj} \\
    \text{s.t.} \;\;
        & g(x_k) + J(x_k)\, \Delta x - (y_k + \Delta y) \leq 0,
    \label{eq:appA_lp_ineq} \\
        & x^{L,\text{cur}} \leq x_k + \Delta x \leq x^{U,\text{cur}},
        \qquad
          0 \leq y_k + \Delta y \leq y^{U},
        \label{eq:appA_lp_box}
\end{align}
where the trust-region box for $\Delta x$ comes from
Section~\ref{sec:slpA:movelim}.  Inequality~\eqref{eq:appA_lp_ineq}
is rewritten in standard form as $A_\text{ub}\,\Delta z \leq b_\text{ub}$
with $A_\text{ub} = [J(x_k)\; -I_m]$ and
$b_\text{ub} = -(g(x_k) - y_k)$, and the LP is solved by a standard
interior-point/simplex routine.  Because $y$ enters with $-I_m$ and
is bounded below by zero, the LP is feasible at every iteration
regardless of the current $g(x_k)$.

\subsection{Quadratic merit function}
\label{sec:slpA:merit}

The slacks must be discouraged from being permanently active, and
the algorithm needs a single scalar on which to backtrack when a
trial step disagrees with the linear model.  Both roles are served
by the quadratic merit of \citet{sorensen2026fminslp},
\begin{align}
    \Phi(x, y)
    \;=\; f(x) + a_F
        \sum_{i=1}^{m}
        \Bigl(\, R\, y_i + \tfrac{1}{2}\, y_i^2 \Bigr),
    \label{eq:appA_merit}
\end{align}
where $R > 0$ is the slack penalty (Section~\ref{sec:slpA:penalty})
and $a_F = \max(|f(x_0)|,\,1)$ is an objective normalisation fixed
at the first FEA evaluation.  The merit gradient with respect to
$z = [x,y]$ is
\begin{align}
    \nabla_z \Phi(x, y) \;=\;
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \nabla f(x) \\[2pt]
        a_F\,(R + y)
    \end{bmatrix},
    \label{eq:appA_merit_grad}
\end{align}
and this is exactly the cost vector used in the LP
objective~\eqref{eq:appA_lp_obj}.  Reusing the merit gradient as the
LP cost couples the linear subproblem to the nonlinear acceptance
test of Section~\ref{sec:slpA:accept}.

\subsection{Step acceptance: filter, sufficient decrease and feasibility restoration}
\label{sec:slpA:accept}

The LP produces a candidate $\Delta z = [\Delta x,\,\Delta y]$, but
does not decide whether the step should be taken.  Acceptance is
handled by three classical mechanisms.

\paragraph{Convergence filter.}
Following \citet{sorensen2015thicknessfilters} and the global filter
of Fletcher–Leyffer–Toint, define
\begin{align}
    h(x, y) \;=\; \max_i \max\bigl(0,\; g_i(x) - y_i\bigr),
    \qquad
    f_{\text{filter}}(x, y) \;=\; \Phi(x, y) / \Phi_0,
    \label{eq:appA_filter_pair}
\end{align}
with $\Phi_0 = \Phi(x_0, y_0)$ used to scale the merit.  A trial
$(h^{*},\,f^{*})$ is accepted by the filter $\mathcal{F}_k$ when,
for every stored pair $(h_j,\,f_j) \in \mathcal{F}_k$,
\begin{align}
    h^{*} \leq \beta\, h_j
    \quad\text{or}\quad
    f^{*} + \gamma\, h^{*} \leq f_j,
    \qquad \beta,\gamma \in (0,1).
    \label{eq:appA_filter_accept}
\end{align}
After an accepted step with $h^{*} > 0$ the new pair is inserted and
any entry it Pareto-dominates is removed; iterates with $h^{*} = 0$
are not stored.  The filter is monotone in the Pareto sense and
does not depend on $R$, so it suppresses the merit-function
oscillations that can otherwise occur when the penalty is poorly
scaled.

\paragraph{Sufficient decrease.}
Filter acceptance is necessary but not sufficient.  Let
\begin{align}
    \Delta\Phi_{\text{act}}
    &= \Phi(x_k,y_k) - \Phi(x_k+\Delta x,\, y_k+\Delta y),
    \\
    \Delta\Phi_{\text{pred}}
    &= -\nabla_z \Phi(x_k,y_k)^{\!\top}\Delta z;
    \label{eq:appA_pred}
\end{align}
the implementation requires the Armijo-type condition
\begin{align}
    \Delta\Phi_{\text{act}}
    \;\geq\; \sigma\, \Delta\Phi_{\text{pred}}
    \quad\text{or}\quad
    \Delta\Phi_{\text{pred}} \leq 0,
    \qquad \sigma \in (0,1).
    \label{eq:appA_armijo}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Feasibility restoration.}
If $x_k$ is infeasible ($V(x) := \sum_i \max(0,g_i(x)) > \varepsilon_\text{feas}$)
and either~\eqref{eq:appA_filter_accept}
or~\eqref{eq:appA_armijo} fails, the trial is still accepted when
it reduces the total violation by at least a fraction $\gamma_v$,
\begin{align}
    V(x_{\text{trial}}) \;\leq\; (1 - \gamma_v)\, V(x_k).
    \label{eq:appA_feas}
\end{align}
This is the restoration phase of filter-SQP methods and fires early
in the LT1292 run, when several utilization constraints are violated
simultaneously.

\paragraph{Backtracking.}
When neither acceptance route succeeds the implementation does
\emph{not} take a fractional step along $\Delta z$, since the LP
already returns the extremum of the linear model on the trust
region.  Instead the move-limit box is contracted
(Section~\ref{sec:slpA:movelim}) and the LP is re-solved, up to a
maximum of $N_{\text{bt}}$ inner trials per outer iteration.

\subsection{Adaptive move limits (trust region)}
\label{sec:slpA:movelim}

The trust-region box
$\bigl(x^{L,\text{cur}},\,x^{U,\text{cur}}\bigr)$ used in
\eqref{eq:appA_lp_box} is updated per component by a history-based
rule.  For each variable~$i$ the velocity sign is
\begin{align}
    s_i \;=\;
    \frac{x_{i,k-1} - x_{i,k-2}}{x_{i,k} - x_{i,k-1}},
    \qquad |x_{i,k} - x_{i,k-1}| > 10^{-10}.
    \label{eq:appA_vel_sign}
\end{align}
If $s_i < 0$ the variable is oscillating between iterates and its
move limit is multiplied by $a_{\text{shr}} < 1$;  if $s_i > 0$ the
variable moves coherently and its move limit is multiplied by
$a_{\text{exp}} > 1$.  When the denominator is too small to be
trustworthy, the move limit is reset to the base value $\delta$.
After the per-variable update, the box is intersected with the
global bounds $x^L, x^U$ and capped at
$\delta \cdot (x^U - x^L)$, so every component is allowed to move
at most a fraction $\delta$ of its physically admissible range.
A floor $\delta_{\min}$ prevents the box from collapsing.  When
the inner search of Section~\ref{sec:slpA:accept} rejects a trial,
the same routine is called with $a_{\text{exp}} = a_{\text{shr}}$,
forcing every component to shrink regardless of its velocity sign.

A per-variable rule is appropriate here because the LT1292 design
variables span more than an order of magnitude --- chord diameters
of order $75~\text{mm}$ alongside brace wall thicknesses of order
$2~\text{mm}$ --- so a single scalar trust radius cannot remain
appropriate for both.

\subsection{Adaptive slack penalty}
\label{sec:slpA:penalty}

The slack penalty $R$ appears both in the LP cost vector
\eqref{eq:appA_merit_grad} and in the merit
\eqref{eq:appA_merit}: in the LP it discourages slack usage and in
the merit it discourages stepping into infeasible regions.  A
static value is rarely satisfactory, so $R$ is grown monotonically
by
\begin{align}
    R_{k+1} \;=\; \min\bigl(a_M\, R_k,\; R_{\max}\bigr),
    \qquad a_M > 1,
    \label{eq:appA_penalty}
\end{align}
whenever the LP slacks remain active
($\sum_i y_i > \varepsilon_{\text{slack}}$), the feasibility
violation stalls ($V(x_{k+1}) \geq 0.95\, V(x_k)$), or every inner
trial of Section~\ref{sec:slpA:accept} is rejected.  The cap
$R_{\max}$ prevents the LP cost from becoming dominated by the
slack term in pathological cases.

\subsection{Termination criteria}
\label{sec:slpA:termination}

Termination is checked at every LP trial point inside the inner
loop.  Three success criteria are evaluated,
\begin{align}
    \|\nabla f(x_k)\|_2 &\leq \varepsilon_g
    && (\text{first-order optimality}),
    \\
    \bigl|\Phi(x_k, y_k) - \Phi(x_{\text{trial}})\bigr|
        &\leq \varepsilon_f
    && (\text{merit-change tolerance}),
    \\
    \|x_{\text{trial}} - x_k\|_2 &\leq \varepsilon_x
    && (\text{step tolerance}),
\end{align}
together with a safeguard $N_{\max}$ on the number of outer
iterations.  Success is reported only when the accepted iterate is
feasible, i.e.\ $\max_i \max(0,g_i) \leq \varepsilon_{\text{feas}}$
and $\max_i y_i \leq \max(\varepsilon_{\text{slack}},
\varepsilon_{\text{feas}})$; reporting success at an infeasible
design would violate the engineering contract of the LT1292
problem.  If the trust-region box collapses below $\delta_{\min}$
without satisfying any of the success criteria, the solver reports
failure: such a stop typically signals finite-difference noise or
a strongly nonlinear active constraint that requires further
attention.

\subsection{Algorithm summary}
\label{sec:slpA:algo}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:slp} collects every ingredient above into one
outer iteration.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{One outer iteration of the implemented SLP solver.}
\label{alg:slp}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Evaluate $f(x_k)$ and $g(x_k) = -c(x_k)$ from MAPDL
       (Eq.~\ref{eq:appA_sign_flip}).
\State Estimate $\nabla f(x_k)$ and $J(x_k)$ by forward FD with
       step~\eqref{eq:appA_fd}.
\State Form the merit gradient $\nabla_z \Phi$
       via~\eqref{eq:appA_merit_grad}.
\State Update the trust-region box via the velocity-sign
       rule~\eqref{eq:appA_vel_sign}.
\For{$j = 1, \dots, N_{\text{bt}}$}
    \State Solve LP \eqref{eq:appA_lp_obj}--\eqref{eq:appA_lp_box}
           for $(\Delta x, \Delta y)$.
    \State Evaluate $f(x_k+\Delta x)$ and $g(x_k+\Delta x)$.
    \State Compute filter pair~\eqref{eq:appA_filter_pair} and
           predicted/actual merit decrease~\eqref{eq:appA_pred}.
    \If{any termination criterion in
        Section~\ref{sec:slpA:termination} holds at a feasible
        current iterate}
        \State \Return \textbf{success}.
    \EndIf
    \If{filter accepts~\eqref{eq:appA_filter_accept} \textbf{and}
        Armijo holds~\eqref{eq:appA_armijo}, \textbf{or}
        feasibility restoration~\eqref{eq:appA_feas} holds}
        \State Accept trial; update $x_{k+1}, y_{k+1}$ and the
               filter; \textbf{break}.
    \Else
        \State Shrink trust region
               (Section~\ref{sec:slpA:movelim}); continue.
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\If{slacks active \textbf{or} feasibility stalled \textbf{or} trial
    rejected}
    \State Grow penalty:
           $R \leftarrow \min(a_M R, R_{\max})$
           \eqref{eq:appA_penalty}.
\EndIf
\If{trust region collapsed without success}
    \State \Return \textbf{failure}
           (Section~\ref{sec:slpA:termination}).
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
